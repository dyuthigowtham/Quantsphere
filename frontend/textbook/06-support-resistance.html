Support & Resistance

The single most-used concept in all of technical trading — everything else tends to build on top of it.

The core idea

  • Support — a price level where buying pressure has historically been strong enough to stop a decline and push price back up. Think of it as a floor.
  • Resistance — a price level where selling pressure has historically been strong enough to stop a rally and push price back down. Think of it as a ceiling.

These levels exist because traders remember them — a lot of orders (entries, stops, take-profits) cluster around previous highs, lows, and round numbers, so price tends to react when it revisits them.

Price oscillating between a resistance ceiling and a support floor — a "range." Traders often buy near support and sell/short near resistance, with a stop just beyond the level in case it fails.

Role reversal — "old resistance becomes new support"

Once price breaks decisively through a level, that same level often flips roles — a broken resistance frequently acts as support on a retest, and vice versa. This retest is a common, lower-risk entry point after a breakout.

How to actually find these levels

  • Prior swing highs/lows — the most basic and reliable source: look left on the chart for where price previously turned.
  • Round numbers — psychological levels like 1.1000 on EUR/USD or 25,000 on Nifty tend to attract orders simply because they're round.
  • Moving averages — a widely-watched MA (e.g. the 50 or 200-period) can act as dynamic, moving support/resistance.
  • Trendlines — connecting a series of rising lows (uptrend support) or falling highs (downtrend resistance) gives you a diagonal, moving level.
💡
Levels are zones, not laser lines. Price rarely respects a level to the exact pip/point. Think of support and resistance as an area a few points wide rather than a single precise number, and give your stops a little room beyond the zone rather than right on it.
