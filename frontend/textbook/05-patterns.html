Chart Patterns

Recurring shapes price tends to make — useful as context, never as a guarantee.

Patterns form because trader psychology repeats: the same crowd behaviors (fear, greed, indecision, capitulation) show up again and again across every market and every era. That's why patterns "work" often enough to be useful — and why they still fail plenty of the time. Treat every pattern below as a probability tilt to combine with support/resistance and risk management, not a signal to trade blindly.

Head and Shoulders (reversal)

Three peaks — a higher middle peak (head) flanked by two lower, roughly equal peaks (shoulders). A break below the neckline signals the uptrend may be reversing down. Upside-down, it's an "inverse head and shoulders" — a bullish reversal signal.

Double Top / Double Bottom (reversal)

Price hits roughly the same high twice, failing to break through — often a sign buyers are exhausted. A break below the support between the two tops confirms the reversal down. A "double bottom" is the mirror image, signaling a bullish reversal.

Ascending Triangle (continuation)

Flat resistance overhead, rising support underneath — buyers are stepping in earlier each time. A break above resistance often continues the prior uptrend. A descending triangle (flat support, falling resistance) is the bearish equivalent.

Flag / Pennant (continuation)

A sharp move ("pole") followed by a brief, contained pullback ("flag") against the move — then a continuation in the original direction. Reflects a quick pause to "catch breath" rather than an actual reversal.
⚠️
Patterns fail — a lot. None of these confirm anything until they actually break the relevant level with real follow-through. Trading the pattern before the breakout confirms is one of the most common ways new traders get caught out. Combine with support/resistance and volume, and always know your invalidation point before entering.
