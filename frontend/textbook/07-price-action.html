Price Action

Reading raw candles and structure without relying on indicators.

"Price action" trading means making decisions primarily from the candles themselves — their shape, size, and position relative to support/resistance — rather than from derived indicators like RSI or MACD. Indicators lag price by definition (they're calculated from price); price action tries to read the rawest, most immediate signal available.

Pin bar (rejection candle)

A small body with a long wick on one side — price pushed hard in one direction and got firmly rejected back. A bullish pin bar (long lower wick) at support, or bearish pin bar (long upper wick) at resistance, is one of the most widely used single-candle signals.

Engulfing candle

A candle whose body completely "engulfs" the prior candle's body, in the opposite direction — a small bearish candle followed by a large bullish candle that fully covers it signals a potential shift in control from sellers to buyers (and vice versa for bearish engulfing).

Inside bar

A candle whose entire high-low range sits inside the previous candle's range — a pause/compression that often precedes a breakout in either direction. On its own it's neutral; context (where it forms relative to support/resistance and the prevailing trend) determines which way the eventual break is more likely to go.

Market structure: higher highs, higher lows

A healthy uptrend prints a staircase of Higher Highs (HH) and Higher Lows (HL). A downtrend is the mirror image: Lower Highs (LH) and Lower Lows (LL). The first Lower Low in an uptrend (or Higher High in a downtrend) is often the earliest hint the trend may be turning — this is the foundation the "smart money" chapter builds directly on.
💡
Price action isn't "no indicators ever" — most experienced traders use a couple of tools (an MA for trend, RSI for momentum) alongside pure candle/structure reading. It's a lens, not a religion: the goal is understanding why a signal might work, not collecting rules to follow blindly.
