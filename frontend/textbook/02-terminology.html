Key Terminology

The vocabulary you'll hit constantly — in this app, in your broker's platform, and in any trading conversation.

Pip
The smallest standard price move for a currency pair — usually the 4th decimal place (e.g. EUR/USD moving from 1.1000 to 1.1001 is 1 pip). For JPY pairs it's the 2nd decimal place.
Lot
A standardized trade size. In forex, 1 standard lot = 100,000 units of the base currency. QuantSphere's "Volume (lots)" field on every trade is this.
Spread
The gap between the bid (price you can sell at) and the ask (price you can buy at). It's effectively the cost of entering a trade, paid to your broker/liquidity provider.
Leverage
Borrowed buying power that lets you control a larger position than your account balance alone would allow (e.g. 1:100 leverage means $1,000 controls a $100,000 position). It magnifies both gains and losses.
Margin
The portion of your own capital set aside as collateral to open a leveraged position. Run out of free margin and your broker issues a margin call or force-closes positions.
Stop-loss (SL)
A pre-set price at which a losing trade automatically closes, capping your downside before it gets worse.
Take-profit (TP)
A pre-set price at which a winning trade automatically closes, locking in the gain.
Slippage
The difference between the price you expected an order to fill at and the price it actually filled at — common during fast-moving or low-liquidity moments.
Liquidity
How easily an asset can be bought/sold without moving its price much. High liquidity (major FX pairs, large-cap stocks) means tight spreads and reliable fills; low liquidity means the opposite.
Volatility
How much and how fast a price moves. High volatility means bigger swings (more opportunity, more risk); low volatility means tighter, slower ranges.
Drawdown
The decline from a portfolio's peak balance to a subsequent low point — the standard way to measure "how bad did it get" independent of overall profit. QuantSphere's dashboard tracks your Max Drawdown automatically.
Risk-reward ratio (R:R)
How much you stand to gain versus how much you're risking on a trade. A 1:3 R:R means you're risking $1 to potentially make $3 — you can be wrong more often than right and still be profitable.
Position sizing
Deciding how large a trade to take, usually as a function of your account size and how far your stop-loss is — the single biggest lever you control for risk. QuantSphere's Pre-Trade Check flags oversized positions before you commit.
Long / Short
Long = bought, betting the price rises. Short = sold (borrowed and sold), betting the price falls.
Bull / Bear
A bull market (or "bullish" move) is trending up. A bear market (or "bearish" move) is trending down.
Trend
The general direction price is moving over a given timeframe — up (uptrend), down (downtrend), or sideways (range/consolidation).
Candlestick
A chart element showing the open, high, low, and close price for one time period. Covered in depth in the Reading Charts chapter.
Support / Resistance
Price levels where buying or selling pressure has historically been strong enough to pause or reverse a move. Covered in depth in its own chapter.
⚠️
Leverage cuts both ways. It's the term most new traders misunderstand — it doesn't just multiply profit potential, it multiplies loss potential by exactly the same amount, and can wipe out an account far faster than an unleveraged position ever could.
