Trading Basics

What trading actually is, the markets you can trade, and how a trade works end to end.

What is trading?

Trading is buying and selling a financial instrument — a currency pair, a stock, a commodity like gold, an index, or a cryptocurrency — with the goal of profiting from its price movement. Unlike long-term investing, which usually means holding an asset for years while a business or economy grows, trading is about capturing shorter-term price swings: minutes, hours, days, or weeks.

Every trade has two sides. If you buy, someone on the other end is selling. If the price moves in your favor, you profit; if it moves against you, you lose. That's the entire game — everything else (analysis, strategy, risk management) exists to tilt the odds in your favor over many trades, not to guarantee any single one.

The markets you can trade

Forex (FX)

Currency pairs like EUR/USD or GBP/JPY. The largest, most liquid market in the world, open 24 hours a day on weekdays.

Stocks / Equities

Ownership shares in a company, e.g. a single stock like Reliance, or a whole index like the Nifty 50 or S&P 500.

Commodities

Physical goods like gold (XAUUSD), silver, or crude oil — often traded as a proxy (futures/CFDs) rather than the physical item.

Crypto

Digital assets like Bitcoin or Ethereum. Trades 24/7, including weekends, and tends to be far more volatile than FX or stocks.

Going long vs. going short

You don't need to think a price will rise to trade it — you're betting on direction, either way:

  • Going long (buying) — you profit if the price goes up from where you entered.
  • Going short (selling) — you profit if the price goes down from where you entered. You're effectively borrowing and selling the asset now, planning to buy it back cheaper later.

In QuantSphere, this is exactly the Buy/Sell choice when you open a trade — Buy for long, Sell for short.

Anatomy of a trade

  1. Entry — the price you open the position at.
  2. Stop-loss — a price below (for a long) or above (for a short) your entry where you'll accept you were wrong and exit, capping your loss.
  3. Take-profit — a price where you'll lock in gains and exit.
  4. Exit — closing the trade, either at your stop, your target, or manually.

Your profit or loss is the difference between entry and exit price, multiplied by your position size (see Lot and Leverage in the next chapter).
