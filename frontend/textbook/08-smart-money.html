Smart Money Concepts (SMC)

A framework built around how large institutional orders are believed to move price — order blocks, liquidity, fair value gaps, and structure shifts.

"Smart Money Concepts" is a popular modern take on price action, built on one core premise: retail-sized orders don't move markets, but large institutional orders do — and those large orders leave identifiable footprints. SMC tries to read those footprints. It's a lens for interpreting the same raw candles you already know how to read, not a different market.

⚠️
Treat SMC as one useful vocabulary for describing price behavior — not a guaranteed map of what "the institutions" are literally doing. Nobody outside the actual desks placing those orders knows their true intent; SMC concepts are inferred patterns, and like every framework in this textbook, they fail plenty of the time too.

Liquidity & liquidity sweeps

Liquidity in this context means clusters of resting orders — stop-losses sitting just beyond an obvious swing high or low, and pending orders from breakout traders. A liquidity sweep (or "stop hunt") is when price pokes just beyond that obvious level — triggering those resting orders — then sharply reverses.

Price briefly breaks below an obvious swing low — where short-sellers' entries and long-holders' stop-losses cluster — then snaps back above it. SMC reads this as liquidity being "grabbed" before the real move.

Fair Value Gap (FVG)

A Fair Value Gap is an imbalance left when price moves so fast that one candle's range doesn't overlap with the candle two bars earlier — leaving a visible "gap" in the price action. The idea is that price often (not always) returns to partially "fill" this gap before continuing.

Candle 1's high and candle 3's low leave a gap that candle 2 (the big impulsive move) jumped straight through. That shaded zone is the Fair Value Gap — often watched as a potential area for price to retrace into before resuming.

Order block

An order block is typically the last opposite-direction candle before a strong, decisive move — e.g. the final down-close candle right before a sharp rally. SMC treats this candle's range as a zone where large buy orders were likely placed, and watches for price to return to it as a potential support zone on a later pullback.

Break of Structure (BOS) vs. Change of Character (CHOCH)

Building directly on the market-structure idea from the Price Action chapter:

  • BOS (Break of Structure) — price breaks the most recent swing in the direction of the existing trend (a new Higher High in an uptrend). Confirms the trend is continuing.
  • CHOCH (Change of Character) — price breaks the most recent swing against the existing trend (breaking below the last Higher Low in an uptrend). Often the earliest structural warning that the trend may be reversing.

Premium & discount zones

Within any recent price range, SMC splits it into an upper half ("premium" — relatively expensive, a zone to look for shorts) and a lower half ("discount" — relatively cheap, a zone to look for longs), typically using a simple 50% midpoint (Fibonacci-style) of the range.

💡
Notice how much of SMC is really support/resistance, market structure, and price action from earlier chapters — dressed in newer terminology, with a specific theory (institutional footprints) layered on top. Understanding the fundamentals first makes SMC click much faster than trying to learn it in isolation.
